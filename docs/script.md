# Chrona Content Script

## Opening

Chrona opens as an interactive journey through time. The first chapter begins with
light and the strange truth that looking into space also means looking into the past.

## Chapter 1: The Light From Yesterday

First, the viewer begins with the Moon. The light delay is small, but it proves
the rule: even nearby objects are not seen instantly.

Then the telescope turns toward the Sun. Sunlight takes minutes to reach Earth,
so the Sun we see is already from the recent past.

Then the telescope points beyond the solar system. A nearby star becomes a
message that crossed years before reaching Earth.

Finally, the telescope turns toward a galaxy. The lesson becomes emotional:
looking far into space means looking far into the past.

Chapter line:

"Every star is a delayed message. Every telescope looks into the past."

## Chapter 2: The Clock That Fell Behind

At ordinary speeds, time dilation is too small to notice. But as velocity approaches
the speed of light, the difference between clocks becomes dramatic.

The chapter uses two controls:

- Velocity as a percentage of light speed relative to Earth
- Time measured by an observer on Earth

The simulation calculates the Lorentz factor:

`gamma = 1 / sqrt(1 - v^2 / c^2)`

The traveler's experienced time is:

`traveler time = Earth time / gamma`

Reference frame note:

At 0% velocity, the spacecraft is not "absolutely still." It is at rest relative to Earth, so the
Earth clock and ship clock match in this simplified simulator.

Chapter line:

"The faster you move through space, the less time your clock carries forward."

## Chapter 3: Gravity Bends Time

Gravity changes how clocks tick. The chapter is titled "Where Time Sinks."

The simulator compares a far-away clock with a local clock near a massive object.
It uses:

`local time = far time * sqrt(1 - Rs / r)`

Presets:

- Earth
- Sun
- White dwarf
- Neutron star
- Black hole

Chapter line:

"Near strong gravity, time does not stop all at once. It sinks."

## Chapter 4: The Shortcut Through Time

The chapter is titled "The Fold That Might Hold."

Wormholes are theoretical shortcuts through spacetime. The section must stay careful:
they are mathematically interesting, but no usable wormhole has been observed.

Chapter line:

"A shortcut through space is not automatically a machine through time."

## Chapter 5: The Problem With Changing The Past

The chapter is titled "The Knot in Yesterday."

Past travel raises paradoxes and competing hypotheses:

- Self-consistent loop
- Branching timeline
- Bootstrap paradox
- Chronology protection

Chapter line:

"The past is not only a place to reach. It is a chain of causes to protect."

## Ending

The final section returns to the central idea: time travel begins with understanding time.
